## Built

**"Could teams select Series and Event Name when creating sessions?"** Yes — done.
`series` is now reference data (IMSA WeatherTech, IMSA VP Challenge, NTT IndyCar,
Indy NXT). Creating a session lets you pick a series, and **the circuit list narrows to
the ones that series actually visits** — IndyCar offers 16 of the 23 and won't offer you
Lime Rock. Existing events are offered for reuse, with "+ New event…" as the escape hatch.

I deliberately did **not** invent a dated calendar (see the API question below).

**"Teams need their own templates — IndyCar will be different than IMSA."** Done.
Templates are per-team and private to your org. Two starter field lists ship, and they
genuinely differ:

| | GT3 / sports car | IndyCar / open wheel |
|---|---|---|
| Corner weights | kg | lb |
| Tyre pressures | bar | psi |
| Unique fields | diff preload, ARB positions, brake ducts | weight jacker, stagger, wicker, boost |

You instantiate one and it becomes **yours** to edit — nothing is shared or locked. Apply a
template to a sheet and it lays the fields out ready to fill.

## Question: is series/track/timing data available via an API?

**Short answer: yes for schedules and results, no for a free official live feed.**

- **IndyCar** — [Sportradar's IndyCar API](https://developer.sportradar.com/racing/reference/indycar-overview)
  provides schedules and post-race results for the series (commercial licence).
  [Blacktop's IndyCar API](https://ocblacktop.com/api/indycar) offers schedules, results,
  driver and team standings and history back to 1996 as JSON, with a free tier around
  7,500 requests/month — that is enough to import a season calendar.
- **IMSA** — results are published through
  [Alkamel](https://imsa.results.alkamelcloud.com/), and the official
  [IMSA app](https://www.imsa.com/mobileapps/) carries the live timing feed teams use.
  There is no advertised public developer API; live timing would need a commercial
  arrangement with IMSA/Alkamel.
- **General** — [Enetpulse](https://enetpulse.com/motorsports-data/) sells a multi-series
  motorsport feed.

**Recommendation.** Do this in two steps, and keep them clearly separate:

1. **Calendar import (cheap, do it soon).** Pull season schedules from Blacktop for
   IndyCar to populate real rounds and dates. `series.calendar_source` already exists to
   record where a round came from, so an imported round can never be mistaken for one
   somebody typed. IMSA's calendar may need to be entered by hand until a feed is licensed.
2. **Live timing (expensive, later).** Only worth it once teams are actually using the
   product. It is a licensing conversation more than an engineering one — and note the
   teams themselves already receive the official feed, so the higher-value near-term
   feature is probably **importing their own timing exports**, not us re-buying the feed.

## Question: "Parts & CAD and Car & Build are confusing"

You're right, and the confusion is my fault — the two screens show fragments without
showing the thing that connects them. **The car should be the spine, and right now it
isn't visible as one.**

What actually exists in the database today:

```
team ── car ── run_session ── laps
   │      └──── part_usage ──── part (PLM: revisions, documents, BOM)
   └──── setup ── setup_values      (per track, optionally per session)
```

So a car already ties to its sessions, its parts and its setups — the UI just never
draws that line. Today **Parts & CAD** is only the life-limit table, and **Car & Build**
is only a list of names.

**What I propose** (not built yet — say the word):

- **Car & Build becomes the car's page.** Pick a car and see: identity and homologation,
  the parts currently fitted with their remaining life, the sessions it has run, its
  setups, and its total running time. That is the "connect the multiple cars to the
  setups and data/components" you were reaching for.
- **Parts & CAD becomes the parts view of the same data** — every tracked part, which car
  it's on, its life, and a link through to the PLM record (revisions, CAD, BOM). Renaming
  it **Service Life** would say what it does.
- A setup sheet should record **which car** it was run on, not just which team. That is a
  one-column migration and it makes "what was on the car when we ran that time" answerable.

## Question: how do teams import existing data — Teamcenter-like layers?

The honest answer: **you already have the layered model; what's missing is import.**

HYPERPLM is structurally close to a light Teamcenter — items with revisions, datasets,
BOM relationships, per-org isolation, an audit trail. Racing data sits on top as its own
domain rather than being crammed into part attributes, which is the right call.

What teams actually hold, roughly in order of how much they'd value having it in one place:

| Data | Format today | Import difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Setup sheets | Excel, one file per session | **Easy** — map columns to a template |
| Lap/timing data | CSV exports from timing software | **Easy** — bulk lap import |
| Telemetry | MoTeC `.ld`, Cosworth, AiM | Hard — binary, better linked than parsed |
| CAD | NX, SolidWorks, CATIA | Already the PLM's job |
| Parts & service life | spreadsheets | **Easy** — CSV import |
| Event/run logs | paper, Excel, notes apps | Easy |

**Recommendation:** build **CSV/Excel import** for setups, laps and parts before anything
clever. It is the difference between a team trying this out and a team actually moving in —
nobody retypes a season by hand. Telemetry should be *attached and linked*, not parsed;
teams already have MoTeC for analysis, and what they lack is the connection between a
telemetry file, the setup that produced it and the lap time it made.

I would **not** build a general "multiple layers of database" abstraction. The layers you
need already exist (org → team → car → session → data), and a generic system would make
every query harder without making anything possible that isn't already.
